In my limited experience, there is a strong sentiment against people who have lost weight by using semaglutide. 
I feel that the majority of the hate against these medicines and its users is ideological in nature and misdirected.
I, however, do not sympathize with people who take these medicines for vanity purposes and not medical.

The first group of people who seem to be the most opposed is the one who is obsessed with being "natural". 
Medication is treated as an alien object which is introduced to destroy nature's perfect harmony inside the 
human body. The extremes of these kind of people are those who refuse to take any kind of medication or vaccine and try 
to treat their ailments via alternative methods which are deemed natural (think homeopathy, ayurveda, naturopathic, etc.). 
Or, not treat it at all. For these people, a medication which brings such drastic visual impact on the human body is 
probably a sin.

The second group which exhibits anti-medication behavior towards semaglutides are those who think that
losing weight or fixing health issues via medication is a lazy way out. They are not anti medication in general, but
medication is the last resort and anything less is cheating. They attach a heavy meaning to the process and believe in the
moralization of effort. Their identity is challenged when they find that there is an easy way to get healthy.

It is hard to reason with the former group of people due to their scientific illiteracy or experiences which makes it 
difficult for them to trust doctors or medicines. 
Meanwhile, the latter group comes across as more accepting and rational, they still do not encourage the use of such 
medicines. Their eyes are clouded by the idea of a perfect journey, often shaming the consumers of semaglutides.
My beef is mostly with this second group of people as I feel they are (culturally) gate-keeping health. 

**People have to realize that there is an industrial scale effort to make people eat junk.** Corporates invest millions 
of dollars to exploit human beings' most natural tendencies and instincts. They employ psychological exploitation via 
engineered packaging and marketing, use technological innovation to enable people to order food with a push of a button, 
without moving an inch, via food delivery apps and chemical engineering to make food addictive. 
Furthermore, there are communities and religious groups which brainwash people to not eat food with enough nutrients 
(vegetarian vs non-vegetarian, pure vs impure). In such a hostile environment, it becomes even more difficult 
to maintain eating healthy.

I believe that as long as the medical and scientific community are in agreement with the right use of these medicines 
and represent it honestly, we, the public, should not gate keep its usage by people who need it. The positive impact 
this wonder drug is making on the lives of millions of people is undeniable. Fixing obesity will fix dozens, if not 
hundreds, of other medical issues. Some patients will get motivated to pursue the "natural" or ideal approach to
metabolic health, some will continue taking the medication as the "lazy" way out, and some will probably relapse post 
treatment and re-gain the lost weight. However, in the end, there will be more healthy people than before when 
semaglutides where introduced to the general population.
